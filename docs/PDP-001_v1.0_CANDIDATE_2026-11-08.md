## 1. Decision statement and mission

**Candidate mission:** Peptide Clarity makes peptide and adjacent-substance science easier to understand, helps visitors distinguish legally approved medicines from compounded drugs and unapproved research materials, and lets visitors inspect source-backed company/lot documentation without presenting unverified laboratory claims as safety certifications.

**Audience hypothesis:** Adults in the U.S. with little technical knowledge seeking credible research and transparent access information. This has **not** been validated in a representative sample. Outside interviews remain deferred; owner-led evaluation is the active process.

**Scope boundary:** Educational research and verified lawful prescription-access information, not individual diagnosis, dosing, personal treatment recommendations, vendor safety guarantees, unapproved human-use shopping links or medical advice. A research company's documentation is a separate evidence channel, not proof of appropriate human use.

## 2. Three visitor pathways and jobs

### A. FDA-approved finished medicines and pharmacy access
Job: “What FDA-approved product contains this ingredient, what is it approved for, and how can I distinguish a licensed prescription route?”
- Substance profile provides separate *product-specific* record(s) with formulation, approval status, indications, authority, source and checked date.
- Licensed pharmacy research is jurisdiction-specific, dated and tied to a real source, with prescription requirement visible.
- Reported prices only with date, currency, quantity, insurance/coupon assumptions and source. No “available today” guarantee.
- FDA approval of one product is **not** approval of every compounded or research listing sharing its active ingredient. NDC numbers alone do not establish approval.

### B. Compounded medications
Job: “How does a compounded preparation differ from the FDA-approved product, and what can actually be verified?”
- The UI identifies compounded medicines as **not FDA-approved finished drugs**.
- Compounding entity, prescription and applicable jurisdictional/legal framework must be sourced and reviewed; no blanket legal or quality claims.
- Do not present compounded options as therapeutically equivalent to approved finished products without adequate product-specific evidence.

### C. Non-FDA-approved research substances and company evidence
Job: “What do companies claim to sell, what do their documents report, and what remains independently unverified?”
- Directory and side-by-side evidence views for companies, reported lot identifiers, analytical document metadata, claimed measurements, source URL/date and material gaps.
- Differentiate company-hosted PDF, document inspected, issuer independently authenticated, sample custody independently confirmed and product scientifically evaluated.
- No seller “safe to inject/use” labels, arbitrary safety scores, unapproved human-use purchase referrals or clinical dosing.
- Seller data is handled fairly, with dated citations and correction/response process.

## 4. Ranked user needs and acceptance criteria

**UN-001 / FR-001:** Explain a substance in plain English before technical details. User identifies substance category, at least one known uncertainty and whether human evidence is present or absent after scripted owner walkthrough; an unreviewed page must not appear medically endorsed.

**UN-002 / FR-004:** Explain COA purpose and limits. User correctly states purity is not sterility, issuer/custody may be unknown, and report may not match actual purchased stock.

**UN-003 / FR-002:** Keep science, approved-product status and lot analytics separate. QA: visiting semaglutide does not label research vials FDA-approved; a compounded form never receives finished-product approval badge; research listing cannot be ranked as human-use safe.

**UN-004 / FR-003:** Every material scientific/regulatory/supplier claim exposes source identity, URL, review status, and checked/observed date; missing values must be visibly unknown. Aim at 100% traceability on any *published material assertion*.

**UN-005 / REQ-013:** Compare research supplier documentary observations without false safety certainty; COA issuer authentication and custody never become true solely because a PDF is linked.

**UN-006 / REQ-011:** Distinguish approved product-specific access from unrelated sellers, pharmacy license geography and prescription requirements. No stale observation shown as “currently verified”.

**UN-007 / REQ-012:** Recognize compounded medicine's nonapproved finished-product status and uncertainty.

**UN-008 / FR-005:** Supplier correction workflow records an original statement, correction request, resolution and dated public revision without paid influence.

**FR-006:** WCAG 2.2 AA remains release target with actual manual and automated testing; do not claim a certified result from a passing structural CI workflow.

**FR-007:** Least-privilege read/publish system, no anonymous access to unpublished evidence, no secret in browser, independent audit and rollback.

## 5. MVP information architecture and owner-led tasks

- Primary routes: **Discover Substances**, **FDA-Approved Medicines**, **Compounded: Understand the Difference**, **Research Companies**, **Compare Evidence**, **How Verification Works**.
- Substance view: “What is it?” → “Why studied/used?” → “What human evidence exists?” → “Risks and unknowns” → “Specific approved products, if any” → “Research company documents, separately” → “Sources and last reviewed”.
- Company view: identity and source date, product-listing documentary data, report/lot mapping, independent verification status, unresolved issues, correction procedure.
- Comparison: one pathway at a time with precise fields and source/date/unknown states; do not rank incomparable products by price or imply approval.
- Owner task scripts: find a catalogue entry in under 3 navigation decisions; explain a compound status; compare two dossiers, find which evidence is unverified; locate an approved product's official indication; identify a compounded option as not approved; trace a material claim to source; request a correction. Measure observed completion and misunderstandings, not statistically representative rates.

## 6. Data and evidence quality

- Reference base currently 84 catalogue entries; 104 aliases, 37 scientific/regulatory claims, 41 sources, five company dossiers, 15 reported lot-substance observations spanning 14 vendor/lot-token candidates, five evidence issues (three Unresolved, one Not assessable and one Not inspected).
- Target schema and migrations in ARCH-001 and POC-001. Distinguish ingredient, particular product, organization, pharmacy license, research listing, seller document, reported lot, inspected report, independently authenticated issuer and reviewed source.
- `unknown` is never automatically promoted to positive. Source-linked and vendor-published are not synonyms for independent review.
- Imported drafts stay in staging until reviewed and specifically published. External source updates go into quarantine, never automatic release.
- Price/license freshness policy must set review interval by field and jurisdiction before displaying “verified” statements; historical observations may be shown dated.

## 7. Editorial, legal, privacy and independence safeguards

- Material scientific health claims: primary sources + qualified scientific/editorial review. Analytics/COA technical interpretation: appropriate analytical review. Regulatory and pharmacy legal context: qualified review of current U.S. rules.
- Organization fairness: evidence-based attribution, original record hash/snapshot, right of correction, no unverified misconduct allegations.
- Funding: no paid placement affects ranking, review scope or content. Disclose conflicts and any sponsorship.
- Privacy: no visitor accounts or personal health documents at MVP, do not collect identifiable browsing/health data, anonymous public read-only views.
- Security/accessibility: RLS plus grants, isolated preview/staging environments, credential separation, restore drills and mobile/VoiceOver QA.

## 8. Risks and assumptions carried from v0.2

Retain R-001 through R-008 and A-001 through A-009 as **OPEN**, subject to evidence. Elevated specific concerns:
- R-009: Compounded product misidentified as FDA-approved or legally accessible.
- R-010: Dated pharmacy licensure misrepresented as current.
- R-011: Seller-hosted certificate mistaken for authenticated and clinically safe product.
- R-012: Company pricing/stock or formulation comparisons conceal inconsistent units or dates.

**DEC-002:** owner-only trials N=1; external interviews deferred rather than claimed complete. Do not use owner observations as representative success statistics. Test counterexamples that falsify product value and expose unsafe misunderstandings.
