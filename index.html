Verify It

Understand a laboratory certificate

A certificate can describe tests on a particular sample. It cannot automatically establish that every vial on sale matches the tested sample or is safe for human use.

Fictional education example

What does this actually mean?

99.6% HPLC area purity relates to an analytical signal under a reported method. It does not mean the vial is 99.6% safe.

Does 99.6% purity mean sterile?
No. Chemical purity and sterility are different questions. No sterility test is shown for this fictional lot.
Why does 12 mg vs. 10 mg matter?
In the sampled material, the reported quantity exceeds the label by 20%. Quantity and purity are separate measurements.
Does a company-posted COA prove the vial in a customer's hand?
No. We'd need to assess the issuer, original document, sampled lot, sample selection and custody, plus other lot-specific information. Even that does not establish clinical safety.
Real file uploads and automated extraction are not yet implemented. Qualified analytical review is required before presenting real certificates as interpreted findings.

Our method

Company claim ≠ independently verified fact

Every finding should connect a particular company, product, lot, document, method, source, and review date. A positive finding in one area never automatically validates another.

Company-published

We saw a claim on the seller's website. Its truth has not been independently established.

Document examined

We opened the referenced record and inspected what it reports. The named issuer may still be unconfirmed.

Independently confirmed

A specific fact has been verified through a described independent process. This label must not be used for untested dimensions.

Not checked / unresolved

We have not examined the claim or resolved a mismatch. This does not mean passed, failed or fraudulent.
