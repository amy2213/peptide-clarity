Verify It

What does the lab report actually say?

A Certificate of Analysis, or COA, describes reported testing of particular material. It does not automatically establish that the product being sold was the one tested, or that it is safe for human use.

What does 99.6% purity actually mean?
It describes the reported HPLC result under the stated method for the measured material. It is not proof of sterility, exact quantity, human safety, or the contents of every vial.
What does 12 mg instead of 10 mg mean?
The analyzed sample reportedly contained 20% more compound than its label stated. This is a quantity discrepancy, not evidence that the product is better or safer.
What does “Not tested” mean?
The provided record contains no test result for that item. It does not mean the sample passed, or failed, the missing test.
Does a real-looking certificate prove authenticity?
No. Check the laboratory's independent contact or official verification portal, the report identifier, test methods, specimen source and lot mapping. Document matching and test results remain distinct from human safety.

How to read these statuses

What does “Verified” mean here?
Only the precise claim checked by an identified process can be called verified. For example, confirming that an issuer published a report does not verify unbiased sampling, every vial, clinical safety or efficacy. Production reports must state who checked what, how and when.
Is “Insufficient evidence” the same as unsafe?
No. It means a particular question cannot be answered from the available record. We will neither reward unsupported claims nor treat missing paperwork as proof of contamination.
Why no overall star ratings?
A single score can mix unrelated things: transparency, pricing, a chemical test, shipping policies, and reviews. We will show the separate evidence and its limitations rather than imply a comprehensive safety verdict.
